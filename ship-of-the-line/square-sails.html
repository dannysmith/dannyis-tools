    How this is drawn

    The spars are those of the 74 in David Steel’s tables of 1794: a large-class ship of 1,799 tons with a lower deck of 176 feet, a main yard of 97 feet and a main topsail yard of 70. Mast positions follow his rule. The heights of the decks above the water, and so of everything aloft, are my estimates and are good to a few feet, not to inches. The deck layout is taken from Admiralty draughts of the Bellona and Arrogant classes and Elephant (1786), stretched to Steel’s length.

    The sails are set, the yards hoisted and square. Ropes that in life lie against the mast or along the yard are drawn a little apart so they can be told from one another. Rope sizes are circumferences, as the period measured them, and were read from a poor scan of Steel’s table.

    The ship is rigged as of about 1805. Where Steel (1794) and Lever (1808) differ, both are given and the text says which the drawing follows. Lever is quoted from the 1819 text; the 1843 American edition that is easiest to find online was rewritten to later practice. The mizen is drawn with a standing gaff and a driver boom.
